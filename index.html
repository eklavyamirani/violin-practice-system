Instant, specific feedback

You improve fastest when you know right away how far off you were and which way. Hearing "something's off" isn't enough.

The needle shows cents sharp or flat. The fingerboard shows roughly how many millimetres to move and in which direction. You also get a warning when you play a wrong note, like F natural instead of F♯.

Scoring the first landing

Real playing gives you no time to fix a note. What counts is where your finger lands before you adjust it.

Each note is scored from the first steady ¼ second you play. In Lock-in mode you then correct the note and hold it in tune, so the last thing you practise is the right spot.

Tightening the target step by step

Mastery learning: pass one level (85% or more) before you tighten the target.

🥉 ±25¢ → 🥈 ±15¢ → 🥇 ±10¢ for each drill. The app suggests the next level for you.

Removing the help over time

Studies of the "guidance hypothesis" show that constant feedback can make you depend on it. Removing it gradually makes the skill stick.

Once you have a medal, turn on Hide fingering (practise remembering where the note is) and Ears first (you only see the needle after you land).

Practise the hard part on its own

Break a hard skill into parts, drill the hardest one separately, then put it back in context.

Shift pairs repeat each down-shift on its own, and the guide notes show the classic route (slide the old finger, then drop the new one). From the top then puts the shift back into a scale, starting high so you shift down while you're fresh.

Mixing and spacing your practice

Switching between drills and spreading sessions over several days feels harder at the time, but you remember more.

Note Hunt gives you random notes from both positions, picking your weakest notes more often. The "Next up" card switches you between drills. Short daily sessions build your streak.

Hear the note before you play it

You can only play in tune if you can hear the right note in your head first.

Hear it first plays each note before you play it. The tonic drone helps you hear how each note sounds against the key. Listen for the ring when the tonic, 4th and 5th are in tune.

A 10-minute daily routine

  1. Tune (30 s): press "Tune from open A" so the app measures against your own tuning.
  2. Warm up (2 min): one Lock-in run of a drill you already have a medal for, with Hear it first on.
  3. Main work (4 min): the "Next up" drill. Play slowly, and notice which way each note misses.
  4. Mix (2 min): Note Hunt in Lock-in mode.
  5. Test (2 min): the main drill in Flow mode with Hide fingering on. This is the honest test.

Complete 3 runs to fill today's dots. Practise every day to keep your 🔥 streak going.
